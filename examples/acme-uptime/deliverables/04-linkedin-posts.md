## Post 3 — Day 3 · Company page or exec

Buyers read your incident history before they sign now.

Not your uptime number — your *communication*. What did you say during the last outage? How fast? Did you edit it quietly afterward?

That shift is why we think the static status page is done as a trust signal. A green dot asks customers to take your word for it. A timeline shows your work:

- when each update was posted, and by whom
- what changed between revisions (diffs are visible)
- the postmortem, attached to the incident it explains

Trust isn't the absence of incidents. It's the record of how you handled them.

## Distribution plan (organic first hour)

- **Post 1** goes out when our decision-makers are online per our own page analytics `[NEEDS INPUT: page analytics — best posting window]`, with **Notify Employees** triggered ahead of the window. This is the once-per-24h lever and a launch is exactly what it's reserved for; don't spend it on posts 2–3.
- **First-hour engagement:** 2–3 teammates ready with *substantive* comments or reshares (a real anecdote, a question answered). No pods, no comment-for-comment swaps, no "great post!" bait — the buying committee we want can smell it.
- **Link placement:** link in first comment for post 1 per current convention — but this is contested folklore; A/B it on our own page (identical post, link-in-body vs. first comment) and let our data set the rule. The first comment must carry context, not just a URL.
- **Engagement reciprocity:** whoever posts commits to replying to every comment within 24h. Launch posts that ignore their comments train the algorithm — and the audience — to ignore the next one.
- **Measurement:** first-60-minute engagement vs. 24-hour reach logged per post (proving the first-hour effect on our account, not assuming it); counts behind every rate → feeds `06-measurement-plan.md`.
