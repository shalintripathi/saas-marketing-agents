## The problem: nobody believes your status page

You know the hour we mean. Something's down. Your team is heads-down in the incident channel, actually fixing it. Meanwhile support is buried in "is it down?" tickets — every one of them asking a question your team already answered in Slack, twenty minutes ago, to each other.

So somebody breaks off from the fix to write a customer update. It comes out stiff, late, and vague, because writing calm public prose at 3 a.m. is nobody's best skill. And it lands on a status page most customers stopped checking long ago, because all it ever showed them was a green dot.

Here's the part that changed: your buyers noticed too. Prospects now read how a vendor communicates during incidents *before they sign*. A status page that's all green, always, doesn't read as reliability. It reads as silence.

## What we built

Incident Timeline makes the incident record the thing customers actually see:

- **A live, public timeline** embedded in your status page — not a colored dot, a sequence of updates people can follow while the incident is happening.
- **Every update stamped and attributed.** Customers see when it was posted and who posted it.
- **Updates are diffable.** When you revise one, the change is visible. No silent edits — that's the point. The timeline earns trust because it can't quietly rewrite itself.
- **Per-incident follow-ups.** Subscribers to an incident get its updates, and its resolution, without watching the page.
- **The postmortem attaches to the same timeline.** When you publish your postmortem, it lands where the incident lived — one URL from first alert to lessons learned.

## Where the updates come from

The same place they already exist: your incident channel. Acme Uptime drafts customer-ready updates from what your team is already saying in Slack. A human reviews, then it publishes — to the timeline, and everywhere your customers listen: status page, email, Slack, RSS.

Across our customers, the median time from incident-channel message to published customer update is **90 seconds** *(fictional demo metric)*. That's the difference between "communication is a task someone has to break off and do" and "communication is a byproduct of running the incident."
